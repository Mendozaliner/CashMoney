### Root Cause Analysis

The TQF was active **74–78% of OOS days** (depending on config), reducing equity exposure by an average of **32–34%**. This is not a regime filter — it's a near-permanent equity reducer.

**Why?** The Lo & MacKinlay (1988) variance ratio VR(q) < 1.0 is *expected* for **daily** equity returns due to microstructure effects (bid-ask bounce, intraday mean-reversion). In their original 1988 paper, Lo & MacKinlay used **weekly** returns — at that frequency, VR > 1 (genuine positive autocorrelation from trend-following). Our implementation applied VR to daily returns, where the microstructure noise dominates.

**All 12 configs** showed the same pattern:
- VR < 1.0 for 72–78% of days (vs expected ~25–50% in trending markets)
- tqf_scale=0.50 configs: End$1k = $5,600–$6,600 (severe wealth drag)
- tqf_scale=0.75 configs: End$1k = $8,400–$9,100 (still far below CRQS $12,367)

The better risk metrics (Sharpe improvement to 1.028, MaxDD improvement to −11.33%) are real but come entirely from reduced equity exposure, not from smarter regime detection.

### Key Research Finding (Publishable Insight)

> The variance ratio test distinguishes trending from mean-reverting markets, **but only at weekly or longer return horizons**. At daily horizons, bid-ask bounce and microstructure effects create a systematic downward bias in VR that renders the test unable to identify genuine trend quality. Future implementations should aggregate returns to 5-day (weekly) windows before computing VR.

This is consistent with Lo & MacKinlay (1988) and the empirical microstructure literature (Hasbrouck 2007).
