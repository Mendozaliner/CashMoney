**E36 — Faber GTAA-5 Revisited (E10 revisit, DBC now 19+ years):**
Faber's 2007 SSRN paper tested equal 20% allocations across five global asset classes
(US equity / international equity / commodities / real estate / bonds), each held only
when above its 10-month SMA, otherwise earning T-bills. E10 (session 7) was discarded
because DBC had fewer than 3 years of history, truncating Fold 1 severely. The ROADMAP
explicitly flagged this experiment for revisit "when DBC has more history." DBC now
has 19+ years (2006-02-06 forward), making the commodity-inclusive backtest fully valid
for the first time.

The 6-config grid covered `sma_window` ∈ {150, 200, 252} × `band` ∈ {0.0, 0.03} to
test the hysteresis improvement identified in the GTAA-4 work (E6 found band=0.03
reduced whipsaw). Hysteresis is implemented as a stateful loop: enter position when
close > SMA×(1+band), exit when close < SMA×(1-band).

Fold results reveal a persistent structure: Fold 1 (2000–2009) is strong (Sharpe
0.889–1.259) — GTAA's diversification shines during the dot-com/GFC era when equity
underperforms. Fold 2 (2010–2019) is weak (0.298–0.486) — the US equity bull market
rendered international equity (EFA), commodities (DBC), and real estate (VNQ) as
persistent drags while SPY dominated. The hysteresis band consistently helps
(w252/b0.03 mean_wf 0.776 vs w252/b0.00 mean_wf 0.635). Best DD was only −13.6%
(better than v2's −20.5%), but the Sharpe mean never reached the 0.844 bar.

Root cause of failure: DBC and EFA are genuine diversifiers in macro-adverse regimes
(stagflation, crisis) but are structural underperformers during the prolonged US equity
bull (2010–2019 and 2020–present). The equal-weight constraint forces 40% exposure to
international equity + commodities even when those regime signals are off — and the
SMA gate exits only AFTER underperformance begins, not before.

**DISCARDED**: mean_wf=0.776 (< 0.844 bar) AND DSR=0.813 (< 0.95 bar). CI straddles
zero. Terminal $3,546 is well below v2 ($8,382). Not watch-list eligible (corr_v2=0.741
exceeds 0.70 threshold — the international equity + commodity exposure ultimately
tracks the same SPY trend that v2 uses).

**E37 — Swensen 4-Asset Diversified Allocation:**
David Swensen's "Pioneering Portfolio Management" (Yale Endowment, ~13.9% CAGR
1990–2009) argues for truly uncorrelated economic premia: equity, duration,
real assets, and inflation hedges. This experiment implemented a liquid ETF version:
SPY (equity) + IEF (duration) + GLD (inflation) + VNQ/DBC (real asset) at 25% each,
each independently trend-gated by SMA(sma_window). The VNQ vs DBC swap tests whether
real estate or commodities adds more value as the 4th premium slot.

The 6-config grid covered `sma_window` ∈ {100, 150, 200} × `use_dbc` ∈ {False, True}.
VNQ configurations showed more consistent fold-2 performance (Sharpe 0.411–0.676)
while DBC configurations had higher OOS Sharpe (0.811–1.005) but catastrophic fold-2
(0.124–0.515). The DBC fold-2 weakness mirrors E36's failure — commodity exposure
dragged during the 2010-2019 US equity bull.

Best config SWN4(w150,DBC): mean_wf=0.748 (below bar), DD=−11.0% (best DD found in
any multi-asset experiment — beats even RP's −7.6% at some configs), DSR=0.923 (barely
below 0.95 bar). The corr_v2=0.504 is the lowest correlation recorded for a truly
independent multi-asset strategy that includes SPY — indicating genuine diversification
from the GLD + IEF slots.

Root cause of failure: the equal 25% constraint penalizes the strategy in bull markets
(where SPY would deserve more weight) and equally in bear markets (where GLD/IEF would
deserve more). The SMA gate turns off underperforming assets but requires the
underperformance to begin first, creating a structural lag in allocation.

**DISCARDED**: mean_wf=0.748 (< 0.844) AND DSR=0.923 (< 0.95). CI straddles zero.
Not watch-list eligible (corr_v2=0.504 marginally above 0.50 threshold).

**Notable observation — the diversification paradox:**
Both E36 and E37 produced meaningfully better worst-drawdown profiles (−11% to −14%)
than v2 (−20.5%), yet their Sharpe ratios and terminal values were substantially lower.
This is the classic diversification-return tradeoff: equal weighting across truly
uncorrelated assets smooths volatility but sacrifices the equity risk premium that
has dominated 2000–2025. V2's concentration in SPY (when trend is ON) is the source
of its superior terminal value; the diversification strategies pay a return cost for
their risk reduction. For a capital-preservation mandate they would be attractive;
for the current growth mandate they are not competitive.
